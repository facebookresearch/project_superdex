# Articulated Actors

Articulated actors represent multi-body systems composed of **rigid links connected by joints**. They are the primary building block for simulating robots, characters, mechanisms, and any structure where rigid parts move relative to each other through well-defined kinematic constraints.

Unlike independent rigid bodies, an articulated actor has a set of **generalized joint coordinates** that determine all link poses through forward kinematics. These coordinates include joint angles and displacements, but rotational joints make their combined configuration a nonlinear manifold.

## Formulation

### Configuration and Kinematics {#generalized-coordinates}

The overall structure of the articulation is a rooted tree, where rigid bodies called links form the vertices of the tree graph, while the edges are joints.
There is also a root joint, connecting the root link to the articulation's root frame, defined by the actor's [`worldFromRoot`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) transform.
Different types of joints have different configurations, e.g., a revolute joint has a single scalar angle parameterizing its possible configurations, while a spherical joint's configuration is defined by a general rotation in $\mathrm{SO}(3)$.
The complete articulated configuration $q$ is then in the product manifold $\mathcal{Q}$ of all the joint configuration spaces:
$$
q = (q_1, \ldots, q_n) \in \mathcal Q~,
\qquad
\mathcal Q = \prod_{j=1}^{n} \mathcal Q_j~,
$$
where $q_j\in\mathcal{Q}_j$ is the configuration of joint $j$ and $n$ is the number of tree joints.
Generalized velocities and solver increments belong to the tangent space $T_q\mathcal Q$; the distinction is nontrivial because of the rotational configurations of spherical and free joints.
Pose updates, differences, and state combinations use manifold-aware operations that are natural extrapolations of those described for [rigid-rotation reconstruction](./rigid_actors.mdx#solution-reconstruction).

To simulate articulations with non-tree topologies, SuperDex Physics introduces compliant penalty constraints for [cycle joints](#closed-kinematic-chains), with a formulation analogous to other [constraints](../constraints.mdx#mathematical-formulation).
As such, they become part of the energy formulation determining the equations of motion, not the kinematic description of the configuration.

Forward kinematics composes the fixed [`worldFromRoot`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) placement with the joint transforms along the tree, mapping $q$ to the world-space rigid transform $F_i(q)$ of each link $i$. The corresponding articulation Jacobian $J_i(q)$ maps generalized velocity $v \in T_q\mathcal Q$ to link velocity $V_i$:
$$
V_i = J_i(q)v~,
\qquad
V_i = (\dot{\mathbf c}_i,\boldsymbol{\omega}_i)~.
$$
Here $\dot{\mathbf c}_i$ and $\boldsymbol{\omega}_i$ are the link's world-space center-of-mass and angular velocities, respectively, using the notation defined for [rigid actors](./rigid_actors.mdx#continuous-model). The transpose $J_i(q)^T$ pulls link-space forces and residuals back to the cotangent space $T_q^*\mathcal Q$. This lets each link use the same rigid-body mechanics as an independent [rigid actor](./rigid_actors.mdx#formulation), while the articulation is solved in its generalized variables.

### Dynamics

Articulated actors specialize the shared [Lagrangian dynamics](../dynamics.md#system-dynamics) by composing each rigid link's mechanics with forward kinematics. If $T_i$ and $U_i$ are the rigid-body kinetic and potential energies of link $i$, the per-link contributions have the form

$$
T_{\mathrm{links}}(q,v) = \sum_i T_i\!\left(F_i(q), J_i(q)v\right)~,
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{links}}(q) = \sum_i U_i\!\left(F_i(q)\right)~.
$$

The discrete incremental potential for the kinetic energy uses the [rigid-actor center-of-mass and rotation discretizations](./rigid_actors.mdx#discretization) computed from each link's $\mathbf c_i(q)$ and $\mathbf R_i(q)$, so $J_i(q)$ enters only when differentiating with respect to $q$ for the residual.

Aside from these link contributions, several other sources can contribute energy and dissipation to an articulation's dynamics.
[Joint inertia](#joint-inertia) adds kinetic energy, [joint limits](#joint-limits) and cycle joints add constraint potentials, and [joint friction and damping](#joint-friction-and-damping) add dissipation.
[Controllers](#controllers), [direct generalized loads](#actuating-without-a-controller), and [transmissions](../transmissions.mdx) contribute through the conservative potential $U$, dissipation potential $R$, or other generalized forces $Q_{\mathrm{other}}$ according to their models.
[Soft-skinned actors](./soft_skinned_actors.mdx#energy-formulations) strongly couple an articulation to a soft body through skinning.
[Contact](../contact.md#formulation) and external [constraints](../constraints.mdx#mathematical-formulation) may couple the articulation to other actors, so their energies and residuals depend on the coupled system state rather than on $q$ alone. SuperDex Physics assembles all contributions and advances the coupled system with the implicit stages described in [Dynamics](../dynamics.md#implicit-stage-problem).

## Joint Types

SuperDex Physics supports six joint types:

| Joint Type | Enum Value | DoFs | Description |
|------------|------------|------|-------------|
| **Free** | `Free` | 6 | Unrestricted motion (3 translational + 3 rotational). Typically used for the root link of a floating-base system. |
| **Spherical** | `Spherical` | 3 | Three rotational degrees of freedom (ball-and-socket joint). Rotation is parameterized as a rotation vector. |
| **Revolute** | `Revolute` | 1 | Single rotational degree of freedom around a specified axis (hinge joint). |
| **Prismatic** | `Prismatic` | 1 | Single translational degree of freedom along a specified axis (slider joint). |
| **Hard** | `Hard` | 0 | Rigidly fuses the child link to its parent. No relative motion. Useful for fixing the root to the world or combining geometry. |
| **Cycle** | `Cycle` | -- | Creates a closed kinematic loop by connecting two links that are not in a direct parent-child relationship. Enforced as a soft spherical constraint. Declared via `cycles`, not `joints`. |

:::note
For joint types with an axis (Revolute and Prismatic), the `axis` field ([C++](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html), [Python](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.ArticulatedJointParams.axis)) specifies the axis in the joint's local frame. It is ignored for other joint types.
:::

## Creating Articulated Actors

An articulated actor is created in a **single call** from parallel `joints` and `links` arrays:

- `joints[i]` is the **inbound joint** connecting `links[i]` to its parent.
- Links are listed **parent-first**: the root is at index 0 with `parentLink = -1`, and every other link's [`parentLink`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedLinkParams.html) is smaller than its own index.
- `joint.parentLinkFromJoint` places the joint frame in its parent link's frame; `link.parentJointFromLink` places the link body relative to its inbound joint frame.
- Cycle-closing joints are declared separately in `cycles` (see [Closed Kinematic Chains](#closed-kinematic-chains)).

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
#include <mochi_physics/mochi_physics.h>
using namespace mochi;

// A 2-link arm: Free root + Revolute child (hinge about Z).
ArticulatedActorParams params;
params.name = "arm";
params.worldFromRoot = TransformRT{Real3{0, 0.2, 0}};
params.joints = {
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Free},
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Revolute,
     .parentLinkFromJoint = TransformRT{Real3{0.1, 0, 0}},
     .axis = Real3{0, 0, 1}},
};
params.links = {
    {.parentLink = -1, .shape = rootShape, .colliderType = ColliderType::Box, .density = 1000_r},
    {.parentLink = 0, .shape = childShape, .colliderType = ColliderType::Box, .density = 1000_r},
};

Actor* actor = scene->CreateArticulatedActor(params, error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
import mochi

# A 2-link arm: Free root + Revolute child (hinge about Z).
params = mochi.ArticulatedActorParams(name="arm")
params.world_from_root = mochi.TransformRT(translation=[0, 0.2, 0])
params.joints = [
    mochi.ArticulatedJointParams(type=mochi.ArticulatedJointType.FREE),
    mochi.ArticulatedJointParams(
        type=mochi.ArticulatedJointType.REVOLUTE,
        parent_link_from_joint=mochi.TransformRT(translation=[0.1, 0, 0]),
        axis=[0, 0, 1],
    ),
]
params.links = [
    mochi.ArticulatedLinkParams(
        parent_link=-1, shape=root_shape, collider_type=mochi.ColliderType.BOX, density=1000.0
    ),
    mochi.ArticulatedLinkParams(
        parent_link=0, shape=child_shape, collider_type=mochi.ColliderType.BOX, density=1000.0
    ),
]

actor = scene.create_articulated_actor(params)
```

## Joint Configuration

### Joint Friction and Damping

Joint friction is configured per joint via [`ArticulatedJointParams::friction`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html) ([`ArticulatedJointFrictionParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html)), which supports viscous damping, Coulomb (dry) friction, and an experimental Stribeck effect.

| Field | Default | Description |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| `viscous` | `0.0` | Viscous friction coefficient [N*s/m or N*m*s/rad]. Produces a force proportional to joint velocity. |
| `coulomb` | `0.0` | Coulomb friction coefficient [N or N*m]. Constant opposing force once the joint is moving. |
| `falloffVel` | `1e-3` | Velocity threshold [m/s or rad/s] for dry friction smoothing. Smaller values are more physical but may reduce stability. |
| `stictionExtra` | `0.0` | **(Experimental)** Extra stiction force [N or N*m], representing the difference between peak static and dynamic friction. |
| `stribeckVel` | `0.0` | **(Experimental)** Stribeck velocity [m/s or rad/s] governing the static-to-dynamic friction transition sharpness. |

For a one-DoF joint, let $v_j$ be its tangent velocity, $\mu_v$ its [`viscous`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html) coefficient, $\mu_c$ its [`coulomb`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html) force, $\mu_s$ its [`stictionExtra`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html), $v_f$ its [`falloffVel`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html), and $v_s$ its [`stribeckVel`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointFrictionParams.html). When $\mu_0=\mu_c+\mu_s>0$, define $r=\mu_c/\mu_0$ and

$$
\phi(x)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{x^2}{v_f}\left(1-\dfrac{x}{3v_f}\right), & x<v_f~, \\
\dfrac{2v_f}{3}+r(x-v_f)+(1-r)v_s\dfrac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}
\operatorname{erf}\!\left(\dfrac{x-v_f}{v_s}\right), & x\ge v_f,\ v_s>0~, \\
\dfrac{2v_f}{3}+r(x-v_f), & x\ge v_f,\ v_s=0~.
\end{cases}
$$

The joint's dissipation potential is

$$
R_j(v_j)=\frac12\mu_v v_j^2+\mu_0\phi(|v_j|)~.
$$

The first branch smoothly increases the dry-friction magnitude from zero to the peak static value $\mu_0$; above $v_f$, it approaches the dynamic value $\mu_c$ according to the Gaussian Stribeck model. If $\mu_0=0$, the dry-friction term is omitted. For a spherical joint, the same construction uses the norm of its rotational tangent velocity. Its discretization follows the shared [incremental-potential formulation](../dynamics.md#incremental-potential-form). Friction is ignored for `Free` and `Hard` joints.

### Joint Limits

Joint limits are defined **per joint** on [`ArticulatedJointParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html) via [`minLimit`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html) / [`maxLimit`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html), and enforced as spring-damper [constraints](../constraints.mdx) whose stiffness and damping are set on the same joint ([`limitStiffness`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html), [`limitDamping`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html)):

- **Stiffness** ([`limitStiffness`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html)): how aggressively limits push the joint back. Default: 100 N·m/rad.
- **Damping** ([`limitDamping`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html)): how quickly oscillations at the limit boundary are suppressed. Default: 0.

For **1-DoF joints** (Revolute, Prismatic), the limit is the scalar limit value multiplied by the joint axis vector. For example, a revolute joint about Z with limits [-1, 1] rad:

```
min_limit = [0, 0, -1]
max_limit = [0, 0,  1]
```

Leave [`minLimit`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html) / [`maxLimit`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html) unset (in Python, `None`) for an unconstrained joint. Spherical joint limits are complex to specify; it is often easier to model them as three co-located revolute joints.

The limits of a live actor are readable via [`GetArticulatedDofLimits`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) / [`get_articulated_dof_limits`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Actor.get_articulated_dof_limits), and the limit [constraints](../constraints.mdx) themselves via [`GetArticulatedJointLimitConstraints`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) / [`get_articulated_joint_limit_constraints`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Actor.get_articulated_joint_limit_constraints).

### Joint Inertia

`inertia` ([C++](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedJointParams.html), [Python](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.ArticulatedJointParams.inertia)) adds a scalar joint inertia coefficient $a_j$. A reflected motor-rotor inertia is one possible use. For a one-DoF prismatic or revolute joint, its kinetic-energy contribution is

$$
T_{\mathrm{joint},j}(v_j)=\frac12 a_jv_j^2~.
$$

This kinetic contribution is discretized using the shared [incremental-potential formulation](../dynamics.md#incremental-potential-form).

For a spherical joint, the continuous contribution is $\tfrac12a_j\Vert\omega_j\Vert_2^2$, where $\omega_j$ is the rotational tangent velocity. Its discrete contribution uses the same manifold-aware [rotational incremental-potential treatment](./rigid_actors.mdx#solution-reconstruction) as a rigid body with isotropic moment-of-inertia tensor $a_j\mathbf{1}$; the scalar parameter does not specify a general $3\times3$ tensor. Units are [kg] for translation DoFs and [kg·m²] for rotation DoFs. Joint inertia is ignored for `Free`/`Hard` joints and can be changed live (see [below](#working-with-articulations-at-runtime)).

## Closed Kinematic Chains

To create a closed loop (e.g., a four-bar linkage), define the tree topology as usual and add **cycle joints** in `cycles`. A cycle joint connects a child link to a parent link that is not its tree-parent, enforced as a soft spherical constraint at a pivot in the child link's frame.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
ArticulatedActorParams params;
// 4 revolute-jointed links forming an open chain ...
params.joints = {
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Revolute, .axis = Real3{0, 0, 1}},
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Revolute, .axis = Real3{0, 0, 1}},
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Revolute, .axis = Real3{0, 0, 1}},
    {.type = ArticulatedJointType::Revolute, .axis = Real3{0, 0, 1}},
};
params.links = {
    {.parentLink = -1, .shape = barShape, .density = 1000_r},
    {.parentLink = 0, .shape = barShape, .density = 1000_r},
    {.parentLink = 1, .shape = barShape, .density = 1000_r},
    {.parentLink = 2, .shape = barShape, .density = 1000_r},
};
// ... closed into a loop by tying link 3 back to link 0.
params.cycles = {
    {.parentLink = 0, .childLink = 3, .jointFromChildLink = TransformRT{Real3{0.1_r, 0, 0}}},
};
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python

# 4 revolute-jointed links forming an open chain ...
params.joints = [
    mochi.ArticulatedJointParams(type=mochi.ArticulatedJointType.REVOLUTE, axis=[0, 0, 1])
    for _ in range(4)
]

    mochi.ArticulatedLinkParams(parent_link=i - 1, shape=bar_shape, density=1000.0)
    for i in range(4)
]
# ... closed into a loop by tying link 3 back to link 0.
params.cycles = [
    mochi.ArticulatedCycleJointParams(
        parent_link=0,
        child_link=3,
        joint_from_child_link=mochi.TransformRT(translation=[0.1, 0, 0]),
    )
]
```

## Skinned Surface

An articulated actor can carry an optional **skinned surface**: a single triangle mesh that is deformed by the underlying links via linear blend skinning. This gives the articulation one continuous surface for collision and rendering, instead of the separate per-link collision shapes.

The skin is a **colliding-only surface** — it detects contact against other actors, but others do not detect against it (the per-link shapes remain the colliders). Attach it by setting [`skin`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) on [`ArticulatedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) before creating the actor.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
ArticulatedActorParams params;
// ... joints and links as above ...
params.skin = ArticulatedSkinParams{
    .shape = skinShape,   // a triangle-mesh shape with skinning weights and link indices
    .layer = "Skin",
};

Actor* actor = scene->CreateArticulatedActor(params, error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
params = mochi.ArticulatedActorParams()

params.skin = mochi.ArticulatedSkinParams(
    shape=skin_shape,   # a triangle-mesh shape with skinning weights and indices

actor = scene.create_articulated_actor(params)
```

### ArticulatedSkinParams Reference

The skin is configured with `ArticulatedSkinParams` ([C++](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedSkinParams.html), [Python](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.ArticulatedSkinParams)).

| Field | C++ Type | Python Name | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| `shape` | [`ShapeHandle`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ShapeHandle.html) | `shape` | Triangle-mesh shape for the skinned surface. |
| `layer` | `DynamicString` | `layer` | Contact layer name for the skin (for contact filtering). |
| `contact` | [`ContactParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ContactParams.html) | `contact` | Contact mechanics parameters for the skin. |
| `boundaryElementType` | `ActorBoundaryElementType` | `boundary_element_type` | Finite-element type for the skin's boundary/contact integrals. Default: `Default`. |
| `boundarySubsampling` | `optional<BoundarySubsamplingParams>` | `boundary_subsampling` | Optional subsampling to reduce contact-integral cost. Best combined with `boundaryElementType = P1Q1`. |

:::note
This surface follows the links via skinning; it is not itself physically deformable. A **deformable** skin is possible, but it requires integrating the articulated actor with soft actors into a [soft-skinned actor](./soft_skinned_actors.mdx).
:::

## Working with Articulations at Runtime

The sections above describe how to *define* an articulated actor at creation time. A live actor also exposes a rich runtime API for introspection, reading and manipulating state, actuation, and live re-modeling. The [Articulations examples](../../examples/articulations/double_pendulum_on_rail.md) tour this whole interface as worked examples. (Controller-based actuation is covered separately under [Controllers](#controllers).)

### Lifecycle and Identity

[`CreateArticulatedActor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Scene.html) returns an [`Actor*`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) and a stable [`ActorHandle`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ActorHandle.html). Each link is a nested rigid sub-actor; enumerate them with [`GetNestedLinkActors`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) and look them up with [`GetActor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Scene.html). Destroying the articulated actor destroys its links (and any constraints on them).

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
Actor* actor = scene->CreateArticulatedActor(params, error);
Span<ActorHandle const> links = actor->GetNestedLinkActors(error);
Actor* endEffector = scene->GetActor(links.back());
scene->DestroyActor(actor->GetHandle());
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
actor = scene.create_articulated_actor(params)
links = actor.get_nested_link_actors()
end_effector = scene.get_actor(links[-1])
scene.destroy_actor(actor)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Introspection

[`GetArticulatedShapeInfo`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) returns [`ArticulatedShapeInfo`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedShapeInfo.html), a one-stop dump of the topology ([`linkNames`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedShapeInfo.html), [`jointNames`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedShapeInfo.html), [`jointTypes`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedShapeInfo.html), [`parents`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedShapeInfo.html), joint axes and DoF layout). [`GetNumDofs`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) returns the total DoF count. Per-DoF limits are available via [`GetArticulatedDofLimits`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), and the limit constraints themselves via [`GetArticulatedJointLimitConstraints`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html).

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
ArticulatedShapeInfo info = actor->GetArticulatedShapeInfo(error);
int numDofs = actor->GetNumDofs();
DynamicArray<Real2> dofLimits(numDofs);
actor->GetArticulatedDofLimits(dofLimits, error);                 // [min, max] per DoF
Span<Constraint* const> limits = actor->GetArticulatedJointLimitConstraints(error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
info = actor.get_articulated_shape_info()   # info.link_names, info.parents, info.joint_types, ...
num_dofs = actor.get_num_dofs()
limits = actor.get_articulated_joint_limit_constraints()
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Reading State (Forward Kinematics)

Read the joint-space pose, the world transforms of every link, and the joint velocities into pre-sized output containers.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
DynamicArray<real> pose(actor->GetNumDofs());
actor->GetArticulatedPose(pose, error);
DynamicArray<TransformRT> linkTransforms(numLinks);
actor->GetArticulatedLinkTransforms(linkTransforms, error);
DynamicArray<real> velocities(actor->GetNumDofs());
actor->GetArticulatedJointVelocities(velocities, error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
pose = mochi.DynamicArrayReal(actor.get_num_dofs())
actor.get_articulated_pose(pose)
transforms = mochi.DynamicArrayTransformRT(len(actor.get_nested_link_actors()))
actor.get_articulated_link_transforms(transforms)
velocities = mochi.DynamicArrayReal(actor.get_num_dofs())
actor.get_articulated_joint_velocities(velocities)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Manipulating State Directly

Set the pose from joint-space DoFs, from link transforms (IK-style), or set joint velocities. Pose-space math composes deltas in the tangent space (so spherical DoFs behave correctly), and the end-effector Jacobian is read from a nested link sub-actor.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
actor->SetArticulatedPoseFromJoints(pose, error);            // joint-space DoFs
actor->SetArticulatedPoseFromLinks(worldFromLinks, error);   // IK-style
actor->SetArticulatedJointVelocities(velocities, error);
actor->AddArticulatedDeltaToPose(pose, delta, outPose, error);
actor->ComputeArticulatedPoseDelta(poseA, poseB, outDelta, error);
Span<real const> jacobian = endEffector->GetArticulatedJacobian(error);  // per nested link
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
actor.set_articulated_pose_from_joints(pose=pose)
actor.set_articulated_pose_from_links(world_from_links=transforms)
actor.set_articulated_joint_velocities(velocities=velocities)
actor.add_articulated_delta_to_pose(pose=pose, delta_dofs=delta, out_pose=out_pose)
jacobian = end_effector.get_articulated_jacobian()   # per nested link
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Controlling Contact

Contact is filtered coarsely by string **layers** and finely by per-actor overrides. The nested link sub-actors let you toggle contact for an individual link (e.g. only an end-effector collides with a target).

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
scene->EnableLayerContactSymmetric("Pendulum", "Ball", false, error);
scene->EnableActorContactSymmetric(
    linkHandle,
    ballHandle,
    true,
    IncludeNestedActors::No,
    error);
bool enabled = scene->IsLayerContactEnabled("EndEffector", "Ball");
int numLayers = scene->GetNumContactLayers();
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
scene.enable_layer_contact_symmetric("Pendulum", "Ball", enable=False)
scene.enable_actor_contact_symmetric(
    link_handle,
    ball_handle,
    enable=True,
    include_nested_actors=mochi.IncludeNestedActors.NO,
)
enabled = scene.is_layer_contact_enabled("EndEffector", "Ball")
num_layers = scene.get_num_contact_layers()
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Mass, Root, and Center of Mass

[`GetMass`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) and the root transform are **whole-articulation** queries. Center of mass and linear/angular velocity are **per-rigid-body** queries, so read them from a nested link sub-actor rather than the top-level articulated actor. (The articulated equivalent of [`SetVelocity`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) is [`SetArticulatedJointVelocities`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html).)

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
real mass = actor->GetMass(error);
TransformRT root = actor->GetRootTransform();
actor->SetRootTransform(root, error);                            // teleports the whole actor
TransformRT com = endEffector->GetCenterOfMassTransform(error);  // per nested link
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
mass = actor.get_mass()
actor.set_root_transform(actor.get_root_transform())
com = end_effector.get_center_of_mass_transform()   # per nested link
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Controllers
