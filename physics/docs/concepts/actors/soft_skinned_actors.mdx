# Soft-Skinned Actors

A **soft-skinned actor** is an articulated actor that combines the articulated skeleton with one or more soft (deformable) volumes rigidly attached to the skeleton. The skeleton provides the overall pose while the soft volumes add deformation — surface-level dynamics, compression, and contact response.

This one actor type spans three flavors of articulation:

1. **Rigid links with soft leaf volumes** — a mostly-rigid articulation with soft volumes attached to selected links (e.g. an articulated robot with soft sensors on its end-effectors).
2. **An elastic skin over the articulation** — a deformable skin wrapping the skeleton that deforms under contact (e.g. a character with an articulated skeleton and a soft body).
3. **A blended skin** — a single surface that is soft and deforming in some regions and rigidly skeleton-following in others (e.g. a hand with soft fingertips).

## Architecture

A soft-skinned actor is not an actor entity of its own; it couples two actor types:

- The **articulated body actor** is the skeleton: rigid links connected by joints (see [Articulated Actors](./articulated_actors.mdx)).
- Each **nested soft actor** is a deformable volume: a tetrahedral mesh with a material model and elastic parameters (see [Soft Actors](./soft/overview.mdx)).

Multiple nested soft actors are supported per soft-skinned actor (`DynamicArray<SoftActorParams>`), so different regions can use distinct meshes or materials. Every step, the soft volumes are skinned to the current skeletal pose, then their deformation is solved on top of that posed configuration (see [Energy Formulations](#energy-formulations)).

### Attaching Soft Volumes to the Skeleton

Each soft volume is attached to the skeleton through a two-step mechanism; both steps are always required. First, a subset of its nodes — the **constrained nodes** — are pinned to the articulation. Second, the soft volume is driven via linear blend skinning by a subset of skeleton links. There are two ways to specify skinning, and every nested soft actor uses one of them:

- **Attach to a link** — name the target link in [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) (1-to-1 with [`softParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html)). The nested soft actor is skinned by a single link, hence skinning weights and indices are not explicitly provided. Contact between the nested soft actor and its attach link is disabled automatically.
- **Skinning data** — leave [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) empty and embed skinning weights in the soft shape, mapping its nodes across multiple links.

### Contact Surface

- By default, contact is computed on the individual soft surfaces. Set [`enableCollidingLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) to also collide the rigid links.
- With a **blended skin** (`skeletonParams.skin`), a single overarching mesh becomes the colliding surface, presenting one seamless surface — deforming where it is backed by a soft region and rigidly following the skeleton elsewhere. See [Blended Skin](#blended-skin).

### The Three Flavors

The flavors listed above are combinations of these two choices:

| Flavor | Soft coverage | Attachment | Colliding surface |
|--------|---------------|------------|----------|
| **1. Rigid + soft leaves** | soft volumes on some links | [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) | soft surfaces + rigid links |
| **2. Elastic skin** | skin spanning the body | skinning data | the soft skin |
| **3. Blended skin** | mixed soft / rigid-follow | attach or skinning data | blended [`skin`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) mesh |

## Energy Formulations

Simulating a soft-skinned actor combines **skinning** — the geometric transform that carries a soft volume along with the skeleton — with the **elastic deformation** solved by the nested soft actor. Three things determine the outcome: how the two compose, how the nested soft actor is pinned to the skeleton, and where each physical energy is evaluated.

### Deformation and Skinning

Using the [soft actor](./soft/overview.mdx) notation, let $\mathbf{X}$ be a node's rest (unposed) position and $\mathbf{u}$ its elastic displacement, so $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{u}$ is the deformed position in the rest frame. The skeleton contributes a **skinning map** $\mathbf{T}_q$ for its current configuration $q\in\mathcal Q$ — linear blend skinning over the [links](./articulated_actors.mdx) that drive the node:

$$
\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{y}) = \sum_{\ell} w_{\ell}\,\mathbf{T}_{\ell}(q)\,\mathbf{y}~,
$$

where $\mathbf{T}_{\ell}(q)$ is link $\ell$'s world transform and $w_{\ell}$ its skinning weight. The two compose into the posed configuration:

- **Pure skinning** $\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X})$ — the volume simply follows the skeleton.
- **Skinned deformation** $\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{u})$ — deform in the rest frame, then skin.
- **Blended skin** $b\,\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{u}) + (1 - b)\,\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X})$ — a per-node weight $b \in [0, 1]$ mixes the deformed and purely-skinned positions ($b = 1$ fully soft, $b = 0$ rigidly following the skeleton). See [Blended Skin](#blended-skin).

### Constrained Nodes

The rigid coupling to the skeleton is enforced by fixing $\mathbf{u} = 0$ on a subset of nodes — the **constrained nodes** — as zero-displacement Dirichlet boundary conditions. Those nodes then sit exactly at $\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X})$, following the skeleton rigidly. This gives perfect rigid attachment robustly and efficiently, with no additional coupling constraints between the two actor types, and is always required (see [Attaching Soft Volumes to the Skeleton](#attaching-soft-volumes-to-the-skeleton)).

### Posed vs. Unposed Energies

Each physical energy can be evaluated at the **unposed** positions $\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{u}$ or the **posed** positions $\mathbf{T}_q(\mathbf{X} + \mathbf{u})$. Evaluating everything posed is the most physically accurate. But since skinning is a geometric — not physical — deformation, some terms are cheaper (and sometimes exact) unposed. The choice is made **per term**: set a term's flag on the parent [`SoftSkinnedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) to evaluate it posed, or on the child [`SoftActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html) to evaluate it unposed.

| Energy | Posed ([`SoftSkinnedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html)) | Unposed ([`SoftActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html)) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Elasticity (`hasStress`) | physically accurate | cheaper — and **exact** when the nested soft actor is rigidly attached to a single link via [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html), since a rigid skinning transform preserves elastic energy |
| Inertia (`hasInertia`) | physically accurate | cheaper, but a cruder approximation |
| Gravity ([`hasGravity`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html)) | physically accurate | not available — keep gravity posed on the parent, or disable it |

Contact is **always** posed: it acts on the world-space surface and is not a choice.

:::caution Energy Flag Rules
- A term must not be enabled on **both** the parent and the child — it would be counted twice.
- [`SoftActorParams.hasGravity`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html) must be `false`: gravity is only ever posed (on [`SoftSkinnedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html)) or off.
:::

## Creating Soft-Skinned Actors

A soft-skinned actor is created in a **single call** to [`CreateSoftSkinnedActor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Scene.html) / [`create_soft_skinned_actor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Scene.create_soft_skinned_actor) from a [`SoftSkinnedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html), which bundles:

- a `skeletonParams` ([`ArticulatedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html)) — the articulated skeleton, built from parallel [`joints`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) and [`links`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) arrays (see [Articulated Actors](./articulated_actors.mdx#creating-articulated-actors)); and
- one or more `softParams` ([`SoftActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html)) — the soft volumes, each a tetrahedral mesh with constrained nodes.

Bind each soft region to a skeleton link with [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) (1-to-1 with [`softParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html)). Name any link you attach to so it can be referenced; contact between a nested soft actor and its attach link is disabled automatically.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
ArticulatedActorParams skeletonParams;
skeletonParams.name = "SoftSkinnedDoublePendulum";
// ... joints and links built as in Articulated Actors; links named
// "UpperArm"/"LowerArm" on layer "Pendulum" ...

// A nested soft actor: a tet mesh with constrained nodes (softShape). hasGravity must be
// false; this uses unposed elasticity (hasStress on the soft), accurate when rigidly attached.
SoftActorParams softParams;
softParams.name = "SoftArm";
softParams.layer = "Soft";
softParams.shape = softShape;
softParams.material.type = SoftMaterialType::NeoHookean;
softParams.material.neoHookean.youngsModulus = 1e4_r;
softParams.material.density = 500.0_r;
softParams.hasGravity = false;
softParams.hasInertia = false;
softParams.hasStress = true;

// Build the soft-skinned actor with posed gravity and inertia.
SoftSkinnedActorParams softSkinnedParams;
softSkinnedParams.skeletonParams = skeletonParams;
softSkinnedParams.softParams = {softParams};
softSkinnedParams.softAttachLinks = {"LowerArm"};  // attach the soft to the lower arm
softSkinnedParams.enableCollidingLinks = true;
softSkinnedParams.hasGravity = true;
softSkinnedParams.hasInertia = true;
softSkinnedParams.hasStress = false;

Actor* actor = scene->CreateSoftSkinnedActor(softSkinnedParams, error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
skeleton_params = mochi.ArticulatedActorParams(name="SoftSkinnedDoublePendulum")
# ... joints and links built as in Articulated Actors; links named
# "UpperArm"/"LowerArm" on layer "Pendulum" ...

# A nested soft actor: a tet mesh with constrained nodes (soft_shape). has_gravity must be
# False; this uses unposed elasticity (has_stress on the soft), accurate when rigidly attached.
soft_params = mochi.SoftActorParams(
    name="SoftArm", layer="Soft", shape=soft_shape,
    has_gravity=False, has_inertia=False, has_stress=True,
)
soft_params.material.type = mochi.SoftMaterialType.NEO_HOOKEAN
soft_params.material.neo_hookean.youngs_modulus = 1e4
soft_params.material.density = 500.0

# Build the soft-skinned actor with posed gravity and inertia.
actor = scene.create_soft_skinned_actor(
    skeleton_params=skeleton_params,
    soft_params=[soft_params],
    soft_attach_links=["LowerArm"],   # attach the soft to the lower arm
    enable_colliding_links=True,
    has_gravity=True,
    has_inertia=True,
    has_stress=False,
)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::note Mixed energy formulation
This example mixes the two [energy formulations](#energy-formulations): gravity and inertia are **posed** (on the top-level articulated actor) while elasticity is **unposed** ([`hasStress`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html) on the nested soft actor). This is valid as long as no flag is `true` on both sides.
:::

:::tip Declarative alternative
The same scene can be authored declaratively as a [prefab](../prefabs.mdx) (`.mochi_scene` JSON) and loaded with `prefab::AddToScene` / [`mochi.prefab.add_to_scene`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/prefab.html#superdex.physics.prefab.add_to_scene). The [Soft-Skinned Double Pendulum](../../examples/articulations/soft_skinned_double_pendulum.md) example ships both forms.
:::

| Field | C++ Type | Python Name | Default | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| `skeletonParams` | [`ArticulatedActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) | `skeleton_params` | -- | The articulated skeleton. See [Skeleton Parameters](#skeleton-parameters). |

| `hasStress` | `bool` | `has_stress` | `false` | Elasticity on **posed** (post-skinning) positions. If `true`, each [`SoftActorParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html) must have `hasStress = false`. See [Energy Formulations](#energy-formulations). |

## Skeleton Parameters

[`skeletonParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) is a full `ArticulatedActorParams` ([C++](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html), [Python](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.ArticulatedActorParams)); see the [parameter reference](./articulated_actors.mdx#articulatedactorparams-reference). Joints, links, cycles, initial velocities, and root placement all behave exactly as for a standalone [articulated actor](./articulated_actors.mdx). Only two aspects are specific to soft-skinned actors:

- **`skin`** ([`ArticulatedSkinParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedSkinParams.html)) is the one field with soft-skinned-specific meaning: it enables the **blended skin** coupling mode. See [Blended Skin](#blended-skin).
- **`worldFromRoot`** places the whole actor. Because each nested soft actor's [`worldFromLocal`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html) is forced to identity, the skeleton root transform is the single source of global placement — author the soft rest mesh directly in the skeleton's rest frame.

To keep the rigid links from colliding with each other or the environment (leaving the soft skin as the contact surface), disable their contact layer. In this scene the links share the `Pendulum` layer:

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
scene->EnableLayerContactSymmetric("Pendulum", "Pendulum", false, error);
scene->EnableLayerContactSymmetric("Pendulum", "Environment", false, error);
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
scene.enable_layer_contact_symmetric("Pendulum", "Pendulum", enable=False)
scene.enable_layer_contact_symmetric("Pendulum", "Environment", enable=False)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Nested Soft Actor Parameters

Each entry in [`softParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) uses `SoftActorParams` ([C++](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html), [Python](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.SoftActorParams)); see [Soft Actors](./soft/overview.mdx) for the full field list. The members that carry soft-skinned-specific requirements:

| Member | Requirement inside a soft-skinned actor |
|--------|-----------------------------------------|
| `hasGravity` | **Must be `false`.** Gravity is driven by [`SoftSkinnedActorParams.hasGravity`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html). |
| `hasInertia` / `hasStress` | Select the unposed side of the [energy formulation](#energy-formulations). Must not both be `true` here *and* on the parent. |
| `shape` | Tetrahedral mesh with **constrained nodes** baked in — the nodes pinned to the skeleton (always required; see [Architecture](#attaching-soft-volumes-to-the-skeleton)). |
| `worldFromLocal` | Forced to identity on creation; author the mesh in the skeleton rest frame. |
| `name` | For a [blended skin](#blended-skin), must match the nested soft actor's blending data in the skin mesh. |
| `material` | Each region may use its own model (e.g. Neo-Hookean) and parameters (density, Young's modulus). |

## Blended Skin

The third flavor uses a **blended skin**: a single overarching triangle mesh that presents one seamless surface for the whole actor — deforming where it is backed by a soft region and rigidly following the skeleton everywhere else. This blended mesh, not the individual soft surfaces, is the colliding surface, so contact faithfully represents the actor's outer shape.

Attach it by setting [`skin`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedActorParams.html) (an [`ArticulatedSkinParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1ArticulatedSkinParams.html)) on [`skeletonParams`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html); the skin mesh must include blending data linking each soft region by [`name`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftActorParams.html). The underlying soft volumes still couple to the skeleton the usual way — via [`softAttachLinks`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/structsuperdex_1_1SoftSkinnedActorParams.html) or skinning data — and always carry constrained nodes.

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
skeletonParams.skin = ArticulatedSkinParams{
    .shape = skinShape,   // triangle mesh with per-nested-soft-actor blending data
    .layer = "Skin",
};
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
skeleton_params.skin = mochi.ArticulatedSkinParams(
    shape=skin_shape,   # triangle mesh with per-nested-soft-actor blending data
    layer="Skin",
)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

See [ArticulatedSkinParams](./articulated_actors.mdx#articulatedskinparams-reference) for the field reference.

## Working with Soft-Skinned Actors at Runtime

Creating a soft-skinned actor returns the top-level articulated actor, so the full [articulated runtime API](./articulated_actors.mdx#working-with-articulations-at-runtime) — [`GetNumDofs`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), [`GetArticulatedShapeInfo`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), [`GetArticulatedPose`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), [`SetArticulatedJointVelocities`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), and so on — applies directly. The returned top-level actor also provides handles to its nested link actors and nested soft actors. Query support depends on the target actor and configuration.

### Nested Link Actors and Nested Soft Actors

Enumerate the nested link actors and nested soft actors through the parent [`Actor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html) and look them up with [`GetActor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Scene.html) / [`get_actor`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Scene.get_actor).

<Tabs groupId="language">
<TabItem value="cpp" label="C++" default>

```cpp
auto links = actor->GetNestedLinkActors(error);
for (auto handle : links) {
    Actor* link = scene->GetActor(handle);  // link->GetType() == ActorType::Rigid
}
auto softs = actor->GetNestedSoftActors(error);
for (auto handle : softs) {
    Actor* soft = scene->GetActor(handle);  // soft->GetType() == ActorType::Soft
}
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="python" label="Python">

```python
for handle in actor.get_nested_link_actors():
    link = scene.get_actor(handle)
    assert link.get_type() == mochi.ActorType.RIGID
for handle in actor.get_nested_soft_actors():
    soft = scene.get_actor(handle)
    assert soft.get_type() == mochi.ActorType.SOFT
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### Queries and Contact

Register queries for nested soft actor data, such as `NodePositions`,
`ElementsDeformationGradient`, and `ElasticEnergy`, on nested soft actors. When
no blended skin is configured, register contact queries, such as
`ContactPoints` and `TotalContactForce`, on nested soft actors as well. With a
[blended skin](#blended-skin), register contact queries on the
articulated actor; contact is reported on the skin surface rather than on the
individual nested soft actors. When link contact is enabled, nested link actors
may also support contact queries. Use [`is_query_supported`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Actor.is_query_supported) to determine which actor supports each query.

The following example does not configure a blended skin, so it registers a
`TotalContactForce` query on the nested soft actor. Call [`scene.step`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/python/api/physics.html#superdex.physics.Scene.step)
before reading the query result. Here, `ball` is a rigid actor placed within
reach of the swinging nested soft actor:

```python
soft_actor = scene.get_actor(actor.get_nested_soft_actors()[0])
force_query = soft_actor.register_query(mochi.QueryType.TOTAL_CONTACT_FORCE)
scene.step(1.0 / 60.0)
force = soft_actor.get_contact_force_from_actor_world(ball)  # net force from the ball
soft_actor.cancel_query(force_query)
```

The C++ equivalents are [`RegisterQuery`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), [`GetContactForceFromActorWorld`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html), and [`CancelQuery`](pathname:///generated/api/v1.0/cpp/classsuperdex_1_1Actor.html).
